12. What are the rules of Double Entry System? Explain.

(8 Marks)

The Double Entry System is a system of accounting in which every business transaction affects at least two accounts. One account is debited and another account is credited with an equal amount. Therefore, total debit is always equal to total credit.

Rules of Debit and Credit

  1. Personal Account: A personal account relates to persons, firms, companies, banks, etc.
    Rule: Debit the receiver and Credit the giver.
    Example: Goods purchased from Ram on credit. Purchases A/c is debited and Ram’s A/c is credited because Ram is the giver.
  2. Real Account: A real account relates to assets and properties such as cash, machinery, furniture, building, etc.
    Rule: Debit what comes in and Credit what goes out.
    Example: Furniture purchased for cash. Furniture A/c is debited because furniture comes in, and Cash A/c is credited because cash goes out.
  3. Nominal Account: A nominal account relates to expenses, losses, incomes and gains.
    Rule: Debit all expenses and losses and Credit all incomes and gains.
    Example: Salary paid in cash. Salary A/c is debited because it is an expense, and Cash A/c is credited.
In short:
Personal A/c → Debit the receiver, Credit the giver
Real A/c → Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out
Nominal A/c → Debit expenses/losses, Credit incomes/gains

11. What is Cash Flow Statement? Describe the importance of Cash Flow Statement.

(8 Marks)

A Cash Flow Statement is a financial statement that shows the inflow and outflow of cash and cash equivalents of a business during a particular accounting period. It explains how cash is generated and used under operating, investing and financing activities.

Importance of Cash Flow Statement

  1. Shows cash position: It shows the actual amount of cash available in the business.
  2. Helps in cash planning: It helps management estimate future cash needs and prepare proper cash plans.
  3. Measures liquidity: It shows the ability of the business to meet short-term obligations such as salaries, creditors and other expenses.
  4. Helps in decision-making: Management can make better decisions regarding investment, borrowing and payment of liabilities.
  5. Shows sources and uses of cash: It clearly explains from where cash has been received and where it has been spent.
  6. Helps evaluate operating efficiency: It shows whether normal business activities are generating sufficient cash.
  7. Useful to investors and creditors: Investors and creditors can judge the ability of the business to pay dividends, interest and debts.
  8. Helps compare financial performance: It helps in comparing the cash-generating ability of the business over different accounting periods.
Conclusion: The Cash Flow Statement helps to understand the liquidity, cash management and financial strength of a business.

10. Differentiate between Stock Dividends and Stock Splits.

(8 Marks)

A stock dividend means distribution of additional shares to existing shareholders as a dividend, whereas a stock split means division of existing shares into a larger number of shares by reducing the face value per share.

Basis Stock Dividend Stock Split
MeaningAdditional shares are issued to existing shareholders as dividend.Existing shares are divided into a greater number of shares.
PurposeUsed to distribute profit in the form of shares instead of cash.Used mainly to reduce the market price per share and make shares more affordable.
Face valueFace value per share generally remains unchanged.Face value per share is reduced proportionately.
Number of sharesNumber of outstanding shares increases.Number of outstanding shares also increases.
Effect on reservesRetained earnings or reserves are transferred to share capital.Generally, there is no transfer from reserves to share capital.
Shareholders’ wealthTotal wealth of shareholders does not immediately change.Total wealth of shareholders also remains unchanged immediately after the split.
Accounting entryAccounting entries are required to capitalize reserves.Usually, no major journal entry is required except adjustment in share records.
ExampleA 20% stock dividend gives 20 additional shares for every 100 shares held.A 2-for-1 stock split converts 100 shares into 200 shares.
Conclusion: Both increase the number of shares held by shareholders, but a stock dividend involves capitalization of reserves, whereas a stock split mainly involves division of shares and reduction in face value per share.

9. What are the factors affecting Capital Structure? Explain.

(8 Marks)

Capital structure refers to the proportion of debt and equity used by a company to finance its long-term activities. An appropriate capital structure helps to minimize the cost of capital and maximize the value of the firm.

Factors affecting Capital Structure

  1. Cost of Capital: A company prefers the source of finance that involves a lower cost. Debt is generally cheaper than equity because interest is tax-deductible.
  2. Financial Risk: Excessive use of debt increases financial risk because interest and principal must be paid regularly. Firms with high business risk usually use less debt.
  3. Cash Flow Position: A company with stable and sufficient cash flows can use more debt because it can easily meet interest and repayment obligations.
  4. Control of the Company: Issue of new equity shares may reduce the control of existing shareholders. To avoid dilution of control, a company may prefer debt financing.
  5. Tax Rate: Interest paid on debt is generally deductible for tax purposes. Therefore, companies with higher tax rates may prefer debt to obtain tax benefits.
  6. Flexibility: The capital structure should provide flexibility to raise additional funds in the future. A company should not use all its borrowing capacity at once.
  7. Market Conditions: When share prices are high, equity may be issued; when interest rates are low, debt may be preferred.
  8. Nature and Size of Business: Large and stable companies can generally obtain debt more easily, whereas small or risky firms may depend more on equity finance.
Conclusion: The choice of capital structure depends on cost, risk, cash flow, control, taxation, flexibility, market conditions, and nature of business.

2. Define Financial Management. Why is Wealth Maximization More Important than Profit Maximization? Explain.

(12 Marks)

Meaning of Financial Management

Financial management is the process of planning, organizing, directing and controlling the financial activities of an organization. It is concerned with the proper procurement and effective utilization of funds to achieve the objectives of the business.

It mainly deals with three important decisions: investment decision, financing decision and dividend decision.

Wealth Maximization and Profit Maximization

Profit maximization means increasing the accounting profit of the firm, whereas wealth maximization means increasing the market value of the firm and the wealth of its shareholders.

Why Wealth Maximization is More Important

  1. Considers Time Value of Money: Wealth maximization recognizes that money received today is more valuable than the same amount received in the future.
  2. Considers Risk and Uncertainty: It takes into account the risk involved in expected future returns.
  3. Long-Term Objective: It focuses on long-term growth and financial strength rather than only short-term profits.
  4. Increases Shareholders’ Wealth: Its main objective is to increase the market value of shares and provide higher returns to shareholders.
  5. Considers Cash Flows: It gives importance to actual cash flows generated by the business.
  6. Clear and Definite Objective: Profit can have different meanings, whereas wealth maximization has a clear objective of maximizing the value of the firm.
  7. Helps in Better Investment Decisions: It helps management select projects that provide higher returns after considering risk and time value of money.
  8. Promotes Sustainable Growth: It encourages decisions that support long-term survival, expansion and stability.

Difference between Profit Maximization and Wealth Maximization

Basis Profit Maximization Wealth Maximization

Time periodMainly short-termLong-term
Time value of moneyIgnoredConsidered
RiskUsually ignoredConsidered
Cash flowGives more importance to profitGives importance to cash flows

Conclusion: Wealth maximization is superior to profit maximization because it considers time value of money, risk, cash flows, long-term growth and shareholders’ interests. Therefore, it is regarded as the main objective of modern financial management.
